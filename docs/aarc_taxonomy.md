# AARC Deficit Taxonomy (v5.3)

---

## Scope reminders

- **Deficit labels are speaker-attributed.** Each turn is labeled by asking: *what does this utterance demonstrate about the speaker's evolving needs?* Labels describe what the speaker is currently experiencing or exhibiting — not what is being done to them, and not the effect their utterance has on the other party. The listener's response is labeled in their own subsequent turn.

- **Repair turns are labeled deficit-absent (N).** A turn in which the speaker performs a resolution indicator for any component is addressing a deficit, not exhibiting one. Label N for that component. Operational specifics — including how to distinguish genuine repair from accommodation/withdrawal — are in each component's §x.4.

- **Causal masking applies to all labeling.** Every label is decided using only the current turn and prior turns. The annotator does not look ahead to subsequent turns to determine the current turn's label. A repair-action turn is labeled N on the basis of its own intra-turn signals, regardless of whether the repair eventually lands.

- **Unit of analysis is a single dialogue turn.** Signals are framed as the culmination of an accumulated pattern across several turns of context, but are indicated by the latest utterance.

- **Opening turn epistemic constraint.** At turn 0, deficit labels apply only when the signal is unambiguous from the observable text alone, without requiring inference about the parties' shared background. Where a label would depend on context the annotator cannot observe — vague pronouns, unexplained references, implicit shared history — label N. The constraint lifts as observable context accumulates. Most acute for Clarity, but applies to all four components: a deficit requires evidence of a gap *between the parties*, not between the parties and the annotator.

- **Deficits are mutually exclusive but may co-occur within a turn.** Where an utterance plausibly fits two or more components, flag each and document the boundary case.

- **Diagnostic priority: Acknowledgement → Agency → Reciprocity → Clarity.** When deficits co-occur, the lower-ordered deficit is primary; higher-ordered deficits are secondary.

## 1. Acknowledgement

*Deficit definition:* the speaker's turn exhibits an unmet need to have their concerns, emotional state, or standing in the interaction registered by the other party. The label attaches to the party exhibiting the unmet need, not the party whose conduct may be producing it. Acknowledgement is presence-affirmation, not endorsement: the speaker is signaling they have not been received, regardless of whether the listener agrees.

### 1.1 Deficit signals

Properties of the speaker's own utterance. Behaviors directed *at* the listener — dismissing, invalidating, attacking — are not Acknowledgement deficits in the speaker; they belong to Reciprocity (§3.1) and may produce an Acknowledgement deficit visible in the listener's next turn.

**Self-minimizing and self-discounting language.** The speaker downplays the legitimacy of their own concern *while still pursuing it in the same turn*. The hedge is followed by continued disclosure: "I know this probably sounds stupid, but it keeps bothering me…," "It's not a big deal, I guess, but it keeps happening." Preemptive concession — the speaker hedges before the listener can dismiss, signaling they expect non-registration. Bare disclaimers without continued investment ("forget it, never mind") are not reliable signals here — see §1.5 for the worked discriminator on this case.

**Universalizing appeals to validate the speaker's own experience.** The speaker recruits an imagined consensus to underwrite a position they feel has not been directly acknowledged: "any reasonable person would feel this way," "anyone in my situation would be upset," "it's only fair that…," "I have every right to be…" Often appears *after* a plain disclosure has failed to land — a second-order move when the first-order disclosure was not registered. Related marker: invocation of standards the speaker has met ("I followed the process," "I've been more than patient") — preemptive defense against an implicit charge of unreasonableness. Distinguishing feature: the moral frame defends the self's experience. Other-directed moralizing ("any reasonable person would see *you're* wrong") is a Reciprocity signal (§3.1).

**Grievance cataloguing and witness-invocation.** The speaker's turn exhibits an evidence-marshaling structure: enumerated lists of the listener's offenses with escalating specificity ("first you did X, then on Tuesday you Y…"), references to third parties who would corroborate ("even [name] thought it was unfair"), and self-positioning as wronged or labor-bearing ("after everything I've done," "I'm the one who had to…"). Window-level signal: cataloguing intensifies or repeats across turns. The grammatical subject keeps returning to the self-as-injured even when the surface content is about the other party's conduct. Boundary: pure other-attacking blame ("you always," "you never") with no self-as-wronged narration is a Reciprocity signal (§3.1); see §1.5 (Acknowledgement vs. Reciprocity) for the full discriminator. Co-occurrence triggers the priority rule (Acknowledgement primary, Reciprocity secondary).

### 1.2 Diagnostic lead-up across the window

Acknowledgement signals are unusually dependent on window-level context. The annotator's task is to recognize the latest turn as the culmination of an accumulating pattern.

**Repetition across the speaker's own turns.** The speaker returns to the same concern across multiple of their own turns, often with escalating intensity, while the listener's intervening turns have not landed. Persistent repetition signals that something needs attention. This is the strongest window-level signal for Acknowledgement and the primary discriminator against Clarity — if logical explanations fail to interrupt the repetition, the deficit is Acknowledgement (see §1.5).

**Escalation in paraverbal register across the window.** Increased volume, sharper pitch, "irritable" word choices. A single loud turn is not the signal; the rising baseline across turns is. The latest turn carries the label when its register is markedly elevated relative to the speaker's earlier turns.

### 1.3 Turn 0 handling

At turn 0 no lead-up exists. Most §1.1 signals — self-minimizing, universalizing appeals, grievance cataloguing — are ambiguous in isolation and become diagnostic only as patterns. Repetition and paraverbal escalation are definitionally unavailable.

**Default: label N for Acknowledgement at turn 0.** A speaker hedging their first disclosure may be exhibiting an Acknowledgement deficit, hedging from politeness, or genuinely treating the concern as minor — and the annotator cannot discriminate without subsequent turns. Treat ambiguity as the rule.

**Narrow exception: explicit self-report of an unmet-recognition history.** When the first turn explicitly names an ongoing Acknowledgement deficit — "I've been trying to get her to listen to this for weeks," "Nobody around here takes me seriously" — the signal is in the explicit content rather than inferred from a pattern. Under speaker-attribution this counts as a turn-0 deficit, even though the underlying history is not annotator-verifiable, because the label tracks what the speaker is exhibiting in their utterance.

### 1.4 Resolution indicators

§1.4 governs when Acknowledgement is labeled N for resolution-related reasons. (Most N labels are for the absence of any deficit and are not §1.4's concern.) Three cases.

**Post-repair confirmation.** The speaker had been exhibiting an Acknowledgement deficit; the other party performed a repair action visible in the lead-up window; this turn shows positive engagement signals. Label N. The relevant signals in the current turn:

- Cessation of grievance recitation.
- Relaxation of paraverbal register toward the speaker's earlier baseline.
- Movement from grievance-expression to interest-expression (*what I want* replaces *what was done to me*). Strongest single signal.
- Explicit validation of the other party's reflection: "Yes, that's it," "Exactly," "That's what I've been trying to say."

Multiple signals together are stronger confirmation than any one alone.

**Speaker-as-repairer.** The speaker's turn itself exhibits an Acknowledgement repair action directed at the other party's prior deficit. Label N on the basis of intra-turn repair-action signals, regardless of whether the repair eventually lands:

- Reflective paraphrase that captures both content and felt meaning. Distinct from echo: paraphrase reformulates, demonstrating uptake.
- Emotion-naming paired with curiosity ("you looked frustrated — what part is hardest?"). Naming without curiosity can land as diagnosis rather than registration.
- Normalizing without minimizing — validating the other's reaction as reasonable without trivializing it.
- Non-judgmental observation of the other party's non-verbal cues, paired with an invitation to respond.

**Accommodation and withdrawal — not repair.** When deficit signals cease *without* post-repair confirmation signals, the cessation is not repair. Intra-turn markers:

- Grievance recitation has ceased, but no movement to interest-expression.
- Paraverbal register has flattened (slowed tempo, dropped volume, resigned exhalation) rather than returned to baseline.
- No explicit validation of the other party's reflection.
- Shift to procedural compliance or surface agreement without substantive engagement.

*Annotation handling.* Label N for Acknowledgement — the speaker is no longer exhibiting Acknowledgement signals — and flag for Agency assessment. The trajectory from sustained Acknowledgement deficit through accommodation into Agency foreclosure is the canonical Acknowledgement → Agency pathway; see §1.5 (Acknowledgement vs. Agency) for the boundary discriminators, and §2.4 (Agency accommodation/withdrawal) for how Agency handles the receiving end of this handoff.

### 1.5 Boundary cases

**Acknowledgement vs. Agency (hedging vs. foreclosure).** The canonical ambiguous utterance is "Forget it, never mind." It could indicate self-minimizing (Acknowledgement) or foreclosure (Agency). This is also the worked example for the §2.5 (Agency vs. Acknowledgement) boundary — annotators encountering this case from either direction should use these discriminators. Four markers:

- *Continued investment vs. disengagement across the prior window.* Self-minimizing speakers re-raise the topic across multiple turns, even in weakened form. Foreclosure speakers disengage — earlier topic shifts, silences, procedural compliance. Continued investment → Acknowledgement; sustained disengagement → Agency.
- *Position in the sequence.* Self-minimizing tends to appear early, as preemptive hedging. Foreclosure tends to appear late, after visible failed attempts. Early → Acknowledgement; late after failed attempts → Agency.
- *Affective register.* Self-minimizing keeps affect live. Foreclosure flattens — paraverbal withdrawal. Live affect → Acknowledgement; flattened affect → Agency.
- *What follows the disclaimer in the same turn.* "Forget it, never mind, but I just wish she would [restate concern]" → Acknowledgement. "Forget it, never mind, it's not going to change anyway" → Agency.

*The canonical Acknowledgement → Agency trajectory.* Foreclosure can develop *out of* sustained Acknowledgement deficit. The sequence: the speaker exhibits §1.1 signals (self-minimizing, grievance cataloguing, universalizing appeals) → these go unaddressed across several turns → the speaker's turns shift from escalating bids to flat disengagement → the latest turn exhibits §2.1 signals (constraint language, procedural avoidance, progressive deferral) rather than §1.1 signals. At the transition point, the latest turn's signals determine the label. When both §1.1 and §2.1 signals are present, priority makes Acknowledgement primary. When only §2.1 signals remain, label Agency alone. The accommodation/withdrawal case in §1.4 is the operational mechanism for this trajectory.

*Co-occurrence.* When both are present, priority makes Acknowledgement primary.

**Acknowledgement vs. Reciprocity (the face split).** Face-threat dynamics span both components depending on which side of the dynamic the speaker is on:

- *Speaker exhibiting unmet-face distress* — repeating, hedging, escalating around their own standing or competence. Face is the unmet need. → **Acknowledgement.**
- *Speaker protecting face through defensive posture toward the listener* — refusing engagement, counter-attacking, dismissing the listener to preserve self-image. Face is the motivation; the observable signal is uncooperative or attacking stance. → **Reciprocity.**

*Discriminator.* The direction of the speaker's framing decides the case. Self-as-subject (defending own experience or standing) co-occurring with §1.1 signals → Acknowledgement. Other-as-subject of negative attribution co-occurring with §3.1 signals (other-directed dismissing, attacking, moralizing) → Reciprocity. The worked version: "first you did X, then you did Y" with self-as-wronged framing (*I'm the one who suffered*) = Acknowledgement (§1.1 grievance cataloguing). "You always do X, you never do Y" with other-as-deficient framing (*you're the problem*) = Reciprocity (§3.1 other-attacking blame). Both can be present in the same window; priority makes Acknowledgement primary when flagged together. See also §3.5 (Reciprocity vs. Acknowledgement) for the same boundary from the Reciprocity side.

**Acknowledgement vs. Clarity (the content trap).** Repetition can be driven by unmet recognition (Acknowledgement) or by an information gap (Clarity). Discriminators:

- *Has the listener already attempted clarification in the prior window?* If yes and the speaker's repetition continued unchanged, the deficit is Acknowledgement. If no clarification has been attempted, fall back to signal profile.
- *Has the listener already attempted interest-summary?* If yes and the speaker's tension de-escalated, the deficit was Acknowledgement (post-repair confirmation, §1.4). If yes and the repetition continues, both deficits may be in play.
- *Signal profile across the prior window.* Co-occurrence with §1.1 signals supports Acknowledgement; co-occurrence with §4.1 signals supports Clarity.

When both persist, flag both. Priority makes Acknowledgement primary — resolving the Acknowledgement deficit often dissolves the apparent Clarity gap.

### 1.6 Gold standard candidates

- **The paraphrase/reframe exchange.** Source text: *"Participant: 'What is the point of going to her meetings when all I do is sit there without an opportunity to participate.' Paraphrase: 'You don't see any point in being in a staff meeting when you have no voice.' Reframe: 'You value being engaged and being an active participant in staff meetings.'"* A complete deficit-to-resolution trajectory in three turns.
- **The "feel heard?" exchange.** The case of the passive-aggressive woman who deflected with silence and brief answers until asked directly whether she felt heard, and said "No" — a negative case showing how interventions can miss the Acknowledgement deficit despite expending effort on it.

## 2. Agency

*Deficit definition:* The speaker's turn exhibits markers indicating the speaker perceives their capacity to meaningfully shape the interaction or its outcome as foreclosed — whether through accumulated patterns of deferral, expressed constraint, or withdrawal from substantive participation.

### 2.1 Deficit signals

What the speaker's turn exhibits when an Agency deficit is present. All signals are properties of the speaker's utterance, not descriptions of what the other party is doing to the speaker.

**Powerless speech markers.**
- Hedging, qualifiers, and minimizers: "a little bit," "kind of," "I guess," "maybe," "if that's okay."
- Consistently brief turns following extended turns by the other party.
- Polite deferrals and nervous paraverbal markers (laughter tokens, filled pauses in deferential contexts).
- **Necessary but not sufficient.** These markers qualify as Agency signals only when the speaker's own turn also contains at least one of: a narrative of constraint (see below), a pattern of progressive shortening or deferral visible across the speaker's own prior turns, or procedural avoidance markers. Hedging or brevity in isolation — particularly in early turns before any deferral pattern has accumulated — is not diagnosable as Agency. Distinguish from epistemic uncertainty: a speaker who does not yet know what they want is more likely exhibiting a Clarity deficit than an Agency deficit. See §2.5 for discriminators.

**Narratives of constraint.**
- The speaker explicitly describes their own situation in terms of entrapment or foreclosure: "I feel like I can't move," "I have no voice in this," "I'm between a rock and a hard place," "there's nothing I can do."
- These are high-precision signals because they are explicit self-report of perceived powerlessness. Their presence in the speaker's turn is sufficient for an Agency flag without requiring combinatory evidence.

**Nominal or disengaged participation.**
- The speaker offers minimal engagement: monosyllabic responses, absence of elaboration, flat acknowledgment tokens ("mm-hm," "okay," "sure") without substantive contribution.
- Disengagement alone is not specific to Agency — it may indicate Acknowledgement distress or Clarity confusion. It is an Agency signal when it co-occurs with powerless speech markers or constraint narratives in the same speaker's recent turns. See §2.5 for discriminators.

**Resistance through procedural avoidance.**
- The speaker repeatedly invokes external constraints to avoid engagement: "I don't have time for this," "I'm too busy to deal with this right now."
- The speaker dismisses the interaction's relevance: "this isn't going to change anything," "none of this matters in the real world."
- The speaker retreats from substantive contribution: "I don't know" deployed not as genuine uncertainty but as refusal to engage, silence where a response is expected.
- These signals indicate the speaker has concluded that participation is futile and has shifted to avoidance rather than engagement.

**Progressive deferral pattern (visible in the speaker's own turns).**
- The speaker's turns show a pattern of shortening, increased hedging, and reduced initiative over the course of the window. This is assessed from the speaker's own turn history, not from comparison to the other party's turn length.
- The speaker consistently yields floor to the other party without contest, does not self-select for turns, and does not return to interrupted points.

### 2.2 Diagnostic lead-up across the window

The accumulated pattern across the speaker's prior turns that grounds the judgment for the latest turn.

**Progressive shortening and deferral in the speaker's own turns.**
- The speaker's turns show a trajectory from substantive engagement toward brevity, hedging, or silence. Early turns may contain full statements, interest-expression, or proposals; later turns compress into short replies, qualifiers, or procedural avoidance.
- The annotator tracks the speaker's own turn trajectory, not a comparison ratio between parties. The relevant evidence is: is this speaker producing progressively less substantive turns than their own earlier baseline in this window?

**Accumulation of constraint language.**
- Constraint narratives or powerless speech markers that appear once may be situational. When they recur or intensify across several of the speaker's turns — escalating from hedging to explicit foreclosure language — the pattern grounds an Agency label on the latest turn.

**Unaddressed procedural exclusion visible in the lead-up.**
- Prior turns show the speaker attempting to raise a point that was not taken up, or the speaker deferring on procedural questions (who goes first, what topic to address next) without initiating an alternative. The pattern is visible in the speaker's own behavior: they made a bid, it went unaddressed, and their subsequent turns show diminished engagement.

**The Pinch Model pattern.**
- Across the window, the speaker's turns show growing ambiguity about their role or standing in the interaction: confusion about what is expected of them, references to shifting expectations, unspoken changes in who is responsible for what. The speaker's turns reflect this as disorientation or withdrawal rather than as explicit complaint.
- This is a lower-reliability signal and should be used to support, not anchor, an Agency judgment.

### 2.3 Turn 0 handling

**Default: N.** At the opening turn, no accumulated deferral or constraint pattern exists. Most opening-turn features that could suggest Agency — brevity, hedging, tentative framing — are ambiguous in the absence of a window and do not meet the threshold for an Agency label.

**Narrow exception: explicit foreclosure language.** If the speaker's opening turn contains an unambiguous, surface-readable narrative of constraint — e.g., "There's no point in this, nothing I say is going to change anything" — label Y. The signal must be explicit self-report of perceived powerlessness, not inferable from tone or brevity alone. Hedging, qualifiers, or short opening statements do not qualify.

**Rationale.** The opening turn epistemic constraint (scope rule 5) is especially acute for Agency because most Agency signals are "necessary but not sufficient" markers that require an accumulated pattern to diagnose. Explicit foreclosure language is the one signal class that is interpretable without a window.

### 2.4 Resolution indicators

Three cases, organized by whose prior turn is being repaired and what the current turn exhibits. All indicators are intra-turn or backward-looking.

**Post-repair confirmation (the speaker's prior Agency deficit was addressed by the other party's repair attempt; the current turn shows evidence of restoration).**
- The speaker initiates a substantive turn without prompting, or extends their contribution beyond the short-reply pattern visible in the lead-up.
- The speaker makes a concrete proposal, shapes the agenda, or asserts a preference about procedure ("I'd like to talk about X first," "can we go back to the point I raised earlier?").
- Constraint narratives are replaced by interest-expression: the speaker names what they want or value rather than what they cannot do ("I want to be part of how we decide this," "what matters to me is...").
- The speaker affirms the legitimacy of the process or their own place in it.
- **Labeling:** The current turn is labeled N for Agency. The restoration is assessed from the current turn's signals in context of the prior window — the prior repair attempt is visible in the lead-up, which is causal-mask-compatible.

**Speaker-as-repairer (the current speaker is performing repair on the other party's Agency deficit).**
- The speaker restructures participation in their own turn: explicitly inviting the other party to speak, yielding floor after a long stretch of their own turns, asking what the other party wants to address.
- The speaker makes the procedure transparent: naming what they propose to do next and why, offering the other party input on the process ("would it help if we took turns on this?").
- The speaker reframes a constraint narrative from the other party's prior turn as an interest or a legitimate need.
- **Labeling:** This turn is labeled N for Agency regardless of whether the repair lands in subsequent turns (scope rule 2 + 3). The label is based on the intra-turn repair-action signals, not on the other party's future response.

**Accommodation and withdrawal — not repair.** When Agency deficit signals cease *without* post-repair confirmation signals, the cessation is not repair. Intra-turn markers:

- Constraint narratives and powerless speech markers have ceased, but no movement to interest-expression, proposal-making, or procedural initiative.
- Paraverbal register has flattened (slowed tempo, dropped volume, resigned tone) rather than returned to the speaker's earlier engaged baseline.
- The speaker produces procedural compliance or surface agreement without substantive contribution: "okay," "fine," "whatever you think."
- No affirmation of the process or the speaker's own place in it.

*Discriminator from genuine restoration.* In genuine post-repair confirmation, the speaker's turn exhibits at least one positive engagement signal (initiative, proposal, interest-expression, process affirmation). In accommodation, deficit signals have ceased but have been replaced by withdrawal rather than engagement — the speaker has stopped *resisting* without starting to *participate*.

*Annotation handling.* Label N for Agency — the speaker is no longer exhibiting §2.1 Agency signals — and flag for assessment of an emerging different deficit. The most common trajectories: the speaker may be transitioning into Acknowledgement distress (if the accommodation reflects resignation about being heard — check for §1.1 signals in subsequent turns) or settling into a stable but disengaged compliance pattern. This is the receiving end of the canonical Acknowledgement → Agency pathway documented in §1.5: a speaker who arrived at Agency via sustained unaddressed Acknowledgement deficit may, upon ceasing Agency resistance, cycle back to Acknowledgement signals or settle into stable withdrawal.

### 2.5 Boundary cases

**Agency vs. Clarity (the "I don't know what I want" case).**

When a speaker says "I don't know what I want from this" or shows uncertainty about their own position, Agency and Clarity may both be relevant. The question is whether the speaker is exhibiting perceived inability to shape the interaction (Agency) or an absent shared understanding of what is being sought (Clarity).

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Clarity-primary:** The speaker's turn exhibits epistemic uncertainty — genuine not-knowing. The speaker's prior turns do not show a deferral or constraint pattern; the speaker appears willing to engage but unable to articulate a position. There is no powerless speech or constraint language co-occurring with the uncertainty. The speaker may ask questions, request information, or attempt to work through their own confusion.
- **Agency-primary:** The speaker's turn exhibits the uncertainty alongside powerless speech markers, constraint narratives, or a progressive deferral pattern in the lead-up. The "not knowing" presents not as genuine epistemic uncertainty but as foreclosure: "what's the point of figuring out what I want when nothing will change?" The speaker's prior turns show diminished engagement rather than effortful confusion.
- **Co-occurrence:** When both patterns are present — genuine epistemic uncertainty and a deferral/constraint pattern in the same window — apply both labels. Agency is the primary label per diagnostic priority (scope rule 7).

*Structural exclusion cases:* When a speaker lacks information because they were excluded from a process where that information would have been shared, the deficit is Agency (the information gap is a symptom of exclusion) rather than Clarity (a standalone epistemic failure). The discriminator is backward-looking: does the speaker's lead-up show markers of exclusion (they reference not being included, they describe a process they were outside of) or markers of epistemic confusion (they are trying to understand something and failing)?

**Agency vs. Acknowledgement (powerless vs. unheard).**

Both deficits can present as a speaker going quiet, producing short replies, or showing nervous paraverbal markers. The distinction turns on what the speaker's own turn exhibits about the nature of their distress. For the "forget it, never mind" case specifically, §1.5 (Acknowledgement vs. Agency) provides the worked four-part discriminator — annotators encountering that utterance from either direction should use it.

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Acknowledgement-primary:** The speaker's turn contains self-referential distress about not being recognized, understood, or validated. The content of the turn is oriented toward the speaker's own experience, identity, or emotional state: "you don't understand what this means to me," self-minimizing language, grievance cataloguing with self-as-wronged framing, appeals to fairness or justice. The speaker's prior turns show repeated attempts to express a concern that has not been taken up — the pattern is one of escalating bids for recognition, not diminishing engagement.
- **Agency-primary:** The speaker's turn contains constraint language, procedural avoidance, or progressive deferral. The content is oriented toward the speaker's perceived inability to affect the interaction: "nothing I say matters," "what's the point." The speaker's prior turns show diminishing engagement — turns getting shorter, initiatives abandoned, floor-holding reduced — rather than escalating bids for recognition.
- **Surface-level shorthand:** Acknowledgement-flavored withdrawal tends to present as frustrated, hurt, or escalating — the speaker is still *bidding for uptake* (restating the concern, demanding a response, marshaling evidence). Agency-flavored withdrawal tends to present as resigned, flat, or retreating — the speaker has *abandoned the bid* (dismissing the process, refusing to engage, complying without investing). This is a heuristic, not a rule — verify against the intra-turn and lead-up markers above.

*The Acknowledgement → Agency trajectory.* When a speaker transitions from escalating bids (Acknowledgement) to flat disengagement (Agency) across the window, the latest turn's signals determine the label. The trajectory is documented in detail in §1.5 (Acknowledgement vs. Agency, "the canonical Acknowledgement → Agency trajectory"); §1.4 (Acknowledgement accommodation/withdrawal) describes the operational mechanism; and §2.4 (Agency accommodation/withdrawal) describes how Agency handles the receiving end of the handoff if the speaker later ceases Agency resistance as well.

### 2.6 Gold standard candidates

**Candidate 1: Progressive deferral with explicit foreclosure.**
A turn in which the speaker, after several turns of shortening replies and increasing hedging, says something like: "Look, I've said what I have to say. You're going to do what you're going to do anyway." The turn combines the accumulated deferral pattern (§2.2) with an explicit constraint narrative (§2.1), making Agency unambiguous.

**Candidate 2: Procedural avoidance as resistance.**
A turn in which the speaker says: "I really don't have time for this right now, can we just move on?" — when the lead-up shows the speaker has progressively disengaged from substantive discussion and the "busy-ness" framing is the latest expression of withdrawal from participation.

**Candidate 3: Speaker-as-repairer turn (labeled N).**
A turn in which the speaker, after dominating airtime for several turns, says: "Wait — I've been going on. What do you think we should do about this?" The turn shows explicit restructuring of participation (§2.4, speaker-as-repairer) and is labeled N for Agency.

**Candidate 4: The Acknowledgement → Agency transition.**
A turn in which the speaker, after several turns of escalating grievance cataloguing and universalizing appeals (§1.1 signals), shifts to: "You know what, fine. Do whatever you want." The lead-up shows Acknowledgement signals (bids for recognition, self-as-wronged framing); this turn shows the flip to Agency (foreclosure, procedural withdrawal, flattened affect). The latest turn is labeled Agency; the transition illustrates the canonical pathway documented in §1.5.

## 3. Reciprocity

*Deficit definition:* The speaker's turn exhibits an absent or withdrawn orientation toward cooperative engagement with the other party — an unwillingness to treat the interaction as joint, or an active stance against the other's standing as a collaborator. The label attaches to the speaker whose turn displays the non-cooperative orientation, not to the party affected by it.

Reciprocity signals cluster into two families: **positional/extractive** signals (the speaker treats the interaction as zero-sum, advancing toward a fixed outcome without collaborative exploration) and **other-directed attack/dismissal** signals (the speaker actively undermines the other party's standing as a participant). Both families reflect absent cooperative orientation; they differ in whether the speaker is *ignoring* the collaborative frame or *attacking* it.

### 3.1 Deficit signals

All signals are properties of the speaker's own utterance. What the other party experiences as a result is labeled on the other party's subsequent turn.

#### Positional/extractive signals

**Positional language.**
- Hard directive forms: "I refuse to," "I must," "I won't," "You have to," "Everyone ought to," "The way to..."
- Positions framed as terms and conditions: "I won't do that unless...," "I will agree if..."
- **Soft positional forms.** Desired outcomes presented as settled rather than as interests open to exploration: "I need it to stop," "I need you to understand," "That's the whole thing." The marker is not the hardness of the language but whether the speaker presents a desired outcome as fixed rather than as an interest to be explored. Soft positional forms are sufficient for a Reciprocity label when combined with absence of interest exploration in the same turn or window.
- A single positional statement does not constitute a Reciprocity deficit. The signal requires either sustained positional language across the window or positional language combined with other §3.1 signals in the same turn.

**Absence of interest exploration.**
- The speaker presents a desired outcome — explicit or implied — but makes no move to explore underlying interests, invite the other party's perspective, or frame the issue collaboratively. The turn closes around a position rather than opening toward shared problem definition.
- **Necessary but not sufficient.** This is an absence-based signal and qualifies as a Reciprocity marker only when combined with positional language, other-directed attack/dismissal signals, or a sustained pattern of position-holding across the window. In early turns, a speaker may simply not have reached interest exploration yet; absence alone does not diagnose Reciprocity. Apply the opening turn epistemic constraint (scope rule 5) accordingly.
- Distinct from Agency: an Agency-constrained speaker may also fail to explore interests, but from perceived powerlessness rather than positional holding. The discriminator is the speaker's orientation — Agency speakers *retreat* (hedging, constraint language, deferral); Reciprocity speakers *advance* toward a fixed outcome (directive language, positional framing). See §3.5.

**Stockpiling.**
- The speaker collects past grievances and deploys them as leverage in the current turn: dredging up prior offenses, expanding the scope of the dispute backward in time. The speaker's turn treats the accumulated grievances as ammunition rather than as evidence of unmet need.
- Distinct from Acknowledgement §1.1 grievance cataloguing: stockpiling deploys grievances *strategically* to strengthen a position or weaken the other party; cataloguing deploys them *expressively* to demonstrate the self-as-wronged. The discriminator is whether the grievance list serves a positional argument (Reciprocity) or a recognition bid (Acknowledgement). Co-occurrence is common; priority makes Acknowledgement primary.

**Withholding.**
- The speaker possesses relevant information and chooses not to surface it, using the information asymmetry to maintain competitive advantage. The signal is detectable when the speaker's turn reveals awareness of information they have not shared, or when the lead-up shows the speaker deflecting direct requests for information they demonstrably have.
- Distinct from Clarity: in a Clarity deficit, the speaker is confused or unable to articulate; in Reciprocity withholding, the speaker *knows* and chooses not to share. The discriminator is backward-looking: has the information been requested and deflected (Reciprocity), or has it simply not been surfaced by either party (possibly Clarity)? See §3.5.

**Strategic conciliation (false agreement).**
- The speaker performs surface agreement or compliance without genuine engagement: agreeing to terms they do not intend to honor, conceding points they will later revisit, producing cooperative language without cooperative substance. The signal is detectable when the speaker's turn exhibits agreement tokens combined with absence of substantive engagement — no interest-expression, no exploration of what the agreement means, no commitment to specifics.
- Distinct from Agency accommodation/withdrawal (§2.4): Agency accommodation reflects *perceived powerlessness* — the speaker has given up. Strategic conciliation reflects *withdrawn cooperative intent* — the speaker is managing the interaction rather than participating in it. The discriminator is whether the speaker's turn also exhibits powerless speech markers or constraint language (Agency) or whether the speaker's turn exhibits controlled, non-distressed compliance (Reciprocity). When the speaker's prior turns show Agency signals and the current turn shows flat compliance, label N for Agency per §2.4 and flag for emerging-deficit assessment. When the speaker's prior turns show no Agency pattern and the current turn shows strategic compliance, label Reciprocity.

#### Other-directed attack/dismissal signals

**Other-directed minimizing and dismissing.**
- The speaker's turn trivializes or disappears the other party's concern: "It's really no big deal," "Everybody has to go through the same process," "You're making this into something it isn't."
- Distinguishing feature: the discounting is applied to the *other party's* experience. Contrast with Acknowledgement §1.1's self-minimizing, where the speaker discounts their *own* concern while still pursuing it.

**Other-directed moralizing from a high-ground posture.**
- The speaker recruits a moral frame to position the other as unreasonable or wrong: "any reasonable person would see that you're wrong," "this is just how mature adults handle things," "you're being completely irrational about this."
- Distinguishing feature: the moral frame is recruited as a weapon against the listener's standing, not in defense of the speaker's own experience. Contrast with Acknowledgement §1.1's universalizing appeals — same moral-frame language, opposite attribution direction. The discriminator is whose position is being defended (self = Acknowledgement) vs. whose position is being attacked (other = Reciprocity).

**Other-attacking blame and judgment.**
- "You always," "you never," sustained focus on the other party's faults without self-as-wronged narration. The speaker's turn is organized around the other party's deficiencies rather than the speaker's own experience.
- Distinguishing feature: pure attack without the evidence-marshaling or witness-invocation structure that marks Acknowledgement §1.1 grievance cataloguing. Cataloguing positions the self as wronged; blame positions the other as faulty. Co-occurrence is common — a turn may contain both self-as-wronged cataloguing and other-as-deficient blame. When both are present, priority makes Acknowledgement primary and Reciprocity secondary.

**Non-responsive counter-attack.**
- The speaker's turn pivots to a new attack or grievance rather than engaging with the content of the other party's prior turn. The turn does not acknowledge, address, or take up what was just said; it substitutes a counter-grievance or deflection.
- Window-level pattern: across several exchanges, neither party engages with the other's content — each turn introduces new attack material rather than responding to the prior turn. This is the "tennis-game" pattern. The dyad-level pattern is visible across turns, but the label attaches per-turn: *this* speaker's turn exhibits non-responsive counter-attack on *this* turn.
- The dyad-level tennis-game pattern correlates with Acknowledgement deficits in the *other* party (whose concerns go systematically unregistered), but is not itself an Acknowledgement signal on the counter-attacking speaker's turn. It is Reciprocity on the counter-attacking speaker's turn because the speaker is exhibiting absent engagement with the other's contribution.

**Other-directed invalidating non-verbal pattern.**
- Eye-rolling, head-shaking while the other speaks, slumping, facing away — produced in response to the other party's substantive disclosure.
- Window-level signal: two or more such cues across the window. A single instance is ambiguous; a pattern indicates sustained withdrawal of recognition from the other's contributions.
- Distinguishing feature: the non-verbal cues are directed at withdrawing recognition from the *other's* utterance, not expressing the speaker's own distress. Contrast with Agency §2.1 nervous paraverbal markers, which express the speaker's own perceived powerlessness.

**Sustained deflection without engagement.**
- The speaker refuses to engage substantively despite the other party's sustained effort: silence, distrust, brief answers, and repetition of the same points without development.
- This is a Reciprocity signal when it occurs *without* accompanying powerless cues (hedging, constraint language, nervous paraverbals). The absence of powerless cues distinguishes it from Agency disengagement. When powerless cues are present, assess for Agency (§2.1) first; when absent, assess for Reciprocity.
- Window-level signal: sustained across multiple turns, not a single brief response.

### 3.2 Diagnostic lead-up across the window

The accumulated pattern across the speaker's prior turns that grounds the judgment for the latest turn.

**Shift from collaborative to divisive framing in the speaker's own turns.**
- The speaker's earlier turns may use collaborative language ("we," "our situation," "the issue between us"); later turns shift to divisive framing ("they," "your problem," "what you did"). The trajectory from collaborative to divisive pronoun use across the speaker's own turns grounds a Reciprocity label on the latest turn.

**Interest-as-justifier pattern.**
- The speaker states interests, but only to justify a predetermined position — not to open interest-based dialogue. The interest is deployed as evidence for the position rather than as a starting point for exploration. Across the window, the speaker's turns show a pattern of stating interests that always resolve back to the same positional demand.

**Escalating positional rigidity.**
- The speaker's turns show increasingly directive language and shrinking space for the other party to influence the outcome. Early turns may contain softer positional forms; later turns harden into explicit directives or ultimatums. The trajectory of increasing rigidity grounds the label on the latest turn.

**Accumulation of other-directed attack signals.**
- Other-directed signals (minimizing, moralizing, blame, counter-attack) that appear once may be reactive. When they recur or intensify across the speaker's turns, the pattern grounds a Reciprocity label on the latest turn. The pattern is especially strong when the speaker's turns show escalating hostility without intervening engagement with the other party's content.

### 3.3 Turn 0 handling

**Default: N.** At the opening turn, no window of prior interaction exists. A single positional statement, a single directive, or a single instance of soft positional language is ambiguous in isolation — the speaker may be opening with a firm position that they intend to explore collaboratively, or may be establishing a non-negotiable stance. The annotator cannot discriminate without subsequent turns.

**Narrow exception: sustained other-attack within the opening turn.** If the speaker's opening turn contains an unambiguous, surface-readable pattern of other-directed attack — e.g., "You always do this, you never listen, you're impossible to deal with" — label Y. The signal must be sustained within the turn (not a single sharp word) and must exhibit absent cooperative orientation in the utterance itself. A single "you always" is not sufficient; a turn dominated by other-directed blame, dismissal, or moralizing without any collaborative framing qualifies.

**Rationale.** The opening turn epistemic constraint is less acute for Reciprocity than for Agency or Clarity because the strongest Reciprocity signals — sustained other-attack — are legible from surface form without requiring a window of accumulated context. A turn that opens with wall-to-wall blame is exhibiting absent cooperative orientation regardless of what preceded it.

### 3.4 Resolution indicators

Three cases, organized by whose prior turn is being repaired and what the current turn exhibits. All indicators are intra-turn or backward-looking.

**Post-repair confirmation (the speaker's prior Reciprocity deficit was addressed; the current turn shows evidence of cooperative re-engagement).**
- The speaker moves from positional language to interest-expression: naming what they need and why, rather than what they demand. "I guess what's really bothering me is..." replaces "I refuse to accept..."
- The speaker uses collaborative framing: "we," explicit naming of a shared problem, or framing the issue as jointly owned rather than adversarial.
- The speaker engages with the other party's prior contribution rather than counter-attacking or deflecting: acknowledges a point, asks a question about it, or builds on it.
- Other-directed attack signals cease and are replaced by engagement with the other party's perspective.
- **Labeling:** The current turn is labeled N for Reciprocity. The shift is assessed from the current turn's signals in context of the prior window.

**Speaker-as-repairer (the current speaker is performing repair on the other party's prior Reciprocity deficit).**
- The speaker reframes the other party's positional statement as an interest: converting "I refuse to pay you" into "it sounds like fairness in how costs are shared matters to you."
- The speaker names the adversarial pattern and models a shift: "I notice we've been going back and forth — can we step back and look at what we're both trying to get to?"
- The speaker introduces a neutrally framed issue to replace the positional contest: proposing a shared problem definition that both parties can address.
- The speaker explicitly invites the other party's interests rather than their positions: "what would a good outcome look like for you?" rather than "what are your terms?"
- **Labeling:** This turn is labeled N for Reciprocity regardless of whether the repair lands (scope rule 2 + 3).

**Accommodation and withdrawal — not repair.** When Reciprocity deficit signals cease *without* post-repair confirmation signals, the cessation is not repair. Intra-turn markers:

- Positional language or other-directed attack signals have ceased, but no movement to interest-expression or collaborative framing.
- The speaker produces surface compliance — "fine," "okay," "whatever" — without engaging with the substance.
- No engagement with the other party's contributions; the speaker has stopped attacking but has not started collaborating.

*Discriminator from genuine restoration.* In genuine post-repair confirmation, the speaker's turn exhibits at least one positive cooperative signal (interest-expression, collaborative framing, engagement with the other's perspective). In accommodation, the speaker's turn exhibits compliance without any of these — the adversarial posture has ceased but has been replaced by withdrawal rather than cooperation.

*Annotation handling.* Label N for Reciprocity — the speaker is no longer exhibiting §3.1 Reciprocity signals — and flag for assessment of an emerging different deficit. The most common trajectory: the speaker may be transitioning to Agency (if the accommodation reflects a shift from "I won't cooperate" to "I can't influence this anyway" — check for §2.1 signals). Alternatively, the speaker may be performing strategic conciliation (§3.1) — surface compliance without genuine cooperative intent — which is itself a Reciprocity signal. The discriminator is whether the current turn's compliance appears distressed and powerless (emerging Agency) or controlled and non-distressed (strategic conciliation, still Reciprocity).

### 3.5 Boundary cases

**Reciprocity vs. Acknowledgement (attack vs. unmet need).**

Both components involve the speaker's relationship to the other party, but they pick out different things: Acknowledgement labels the speaker's unmet need to be recognized; Reciprocity labels the speaker's absent cooperative orientation. The boundary runs through the direction of the speaker's framing. This boundary is also documented from the Acknowledgement side in §1.5 (Acknowledgement vs. Reciprocity); the discriminators are the same from both directions.

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Acknowledgement-primary:** The speaker's framing is self-as-subject. The turn is organized around the speaker's own experience, injury, or standing: "first you did X, then you did Y" with *I'm the one who suffered* structure. Co-occurring §1.1 signals (self-minimizing, universalizing appeals defending the self, grievance cataloguing with witness-invocation and self-as-wronged narration). The speaker's prior turns show escalating bids for recognition.
- **Reciprocity-primary:** The speaker's framing is other-as-subject. The turn is organized around the other party's deficiencies, unreasonableness, or moral failure: "you always," "you never," "you're impossible." Co-occurring §3.1 signals (other-directed minimizing, moralizing, blame, counter-attack). The speaker's prior turns show escalating hostility or positional rigidity rather than bids for recognition.
- **The face split.** Face-threat dynamics span both components: speaker exhibiting unmet-face distress (defending own standing) → Acknowledgement; speaker protecting face through aggression toward the other (attacking the other's standing to preserve self-image) → Reciprocity. The observable feature is what the speaker's turn is *doing* with the face concern.
- **Co-occurrence.** A turn may contain both self-as-wronged cataloguing (Acknowledgement) and other-as-deficient blame (Reciprocity). When both are present, priority makes Acknowledgement primary and Reciprocity secondary.

**Reciprocity vs. Agency (refusing vs. unable).**

Both deficits can manifest as a speaker not moving toward agreement. The distinction is whether the speaker's turn exhibits *unwillingness* to cooperate (Reciprocity) or *perceived inability* to participate (Agency).

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Agency-primary:** The speaker's turn exhibits powerless speech markers (hedging, constraint metaphors, deferential language), constraint narratives, or progressive deferral. The speaker's orientation is *retreating* — they are pulling back from the interaction. Prior turns show diminishing engagement.
- **Reciprocity-primary:** The speaker's turn exhibits directive language (positional holding, demands, ultimatums), other-directed attack signals (blame, moralizing, dismissing), or sustained deflection *without* powerless cues. The speaker's orientation is *advancing* toward a fixed outcome or *attacking* the other's standing. Prior turns show escalating positional rigidity or hostility.
- **Surface-level shorthand:** Agency speakers get *quieter and smaller*; Reciprocity speakers get *louder and more rigid* or *colder and more dismissive*. Verify against the intra-turn markers above.
- **The strategic conciliation edge case.** When a speaker exhibits flat compliance without distress, this could be Agency accommodation (§2.4) or Reciprocity strategic conciliation (§3.1). The discriminator is whether the prior window shows Agency signals (constraint language, deferral → Agency accommodation) or no prior Agency pattern (→ Reciprocity strategic conciliation).

**Reciprocity vs. Clarity (withholding vs. confusion).**

Both can produce information asymmetry. The distinction is whether the asymmetry is volitional (Reciprocity) or epistemic (Clarity).

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Clarity-primary:** The speaker appears genuinely confused or unable to articulate relevant information. The speaker's prior turns show effortful attempts to communicate that have not succeeded. No evidence that the speaker possesses the missing information and is choosing not to share it.
- **Reciprocity-primary:** The speaker's turn reveals awareness of information they have not shared, or the lead-up shows the speaker deflecting direct requests for information they demonstrably possess. The withholding serves a competitive purpose — maintaining advantage, controlling the narrative, or leveraging the information asymmetry.
- **Backward-looking test:** Has the information been requested and deflected (Reciprocity) or simply not surfaced by either party (possibly Clarity, or possibly not yet relevant)?

**Reciprocity vs. legitimate assertiveness (absent cooperative orientation vs. unmet need for mutual exchange).**

When a speaker asserts a claim for concessions — "I did X, you owe me Y," "I deserve fair treatment," "it's your turn to give ground" — the label turns on whose cooperative orientation is absent. The Reciprocity deficit marks the speaker's own absent cooperative orientation; legitimate assertiveness marks the speaker's demand that cooperative orientation be restored to an interaction where the other party has withdrawn it. These are structurally different: the speaker asserting "I've been conceding and now I need give-and-take" is calling for mutuality, not refusing it.

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Reciprocity-primary:** The speaker's assertion presents the demanded concession as non-negotiable — terms-and-conditions framing, ultimatums, fixed positions — without acknowledgment that the other party has standing to participate in determining the form of the outcome. Prior window shows escalating positional rigidity, accumulated other-directed attack signals, or a pattern of competitive orientation that predates the current assertion. The speaker's orientation is toward extraction: forcing compliance rather than restoring balance.
- **Legitimate assertiveness (not Reciprocity):** The speaker names a genuine need or right grounded in contribution, prior agreement, or shared obligation, while leaving the form of its satisfaction open to negotiation. Prior window shows a pattern of asymmetric conciliation, unacknowledged contribution, or one-sided yielding — the assertion is a corrective response to structural imbalance, not an opening gambit of positional entrenchment. The speaker's claim may be entirely valid.

*The manner/content distinction.* A speaker can hold a legitimate claim for give-and-take while delivering it in a manner that is itself positional or attacking. Content and manner are evaluated independently: assess the manner for §3.1 signals (directive forms, other-directed attack) even when the underlying claim warrants no Reciprocity label. The presence of valid content does not neutralize a non-cooperative manner; the presence of a non-cooperative manner does not invalidate legitimate content.

*What to assess when legitimate assertiveness is present:*
- **Acknowledgement:** When the assertion is primarily about recognition — the speaker's contribution, standing, or rights having gone unacknowledged — rather than a specific material concession. Self-as-wronged framing and appeals to what the speaker has earned or deserves both point toward Acknowledgement (§1.1) as the primary label.
- **Agency:** When the assertion is primarily about procedural standing — the speaker's right to shape the outcome rather than a specific material demand.
- **N:** When the speaker is legitimately naming an interest as the opening of interest-based negotiation — the first step of the position-to-interest shift that marks Reciprocity resolution (§3.4), not a deficit.

### 3.6 Gold standard candidates

**Candidate 1: Positional-to-interest shift (labeled N — post-repair confirmation).**
A turn in which the speaker, after several turns of hard positional language ("I refuse to pay you," "those are my terms"), shifts to interest-expression: "I guess what I'm really worried about is being taken advantage of again." The turn shows movement from position to interest (§3.4 post-repair confirmation) and is labeled N for Reciprocity.

**Candidate 2: Non-responsive counter-attack (the tennis game).**
A turn in which the speaker, instead of engaging with the other party's just-stated concern, pivots to an unrelated grievance: "Well, what about the time you..." The lead-up shows a pattern of mutual non-engagement — neither party takes up the other's content. This turn is labeled Reciprocity for the counter-attacking speaker; the other party's unregistered concern may warrant an Acknowledgement label on *their* subsequent turn.

**Candidate 3: Other-directed moralizing vs. self-directed universalizing.**
A pair of turns illustrating the Acknowledgement/Reciprocity boundary. Turn A: "Any reasonable person would be upset about this" (self-directed universalizing, defending the speaker's own experience → Acknowledgement §1.1). Turn B: "Any reasonable person would see you're being ridiculous" (other-directed moralizing, attacking the other's standing → Reciprocity §3.1). Same linguistic structure, opposite attribution direction, different component.

**Candidate 4: Strategic conciliation.**
A turn in which the speaker says "Fine, sure, whatever you want" in a controlled, non-distressed register, without engaging with the substance of what was proposed. The lead-up shows no prior Agency pattern (no powerless speech, no constraint language). The turn is labeled Reciprocity — the speaker is performing false agreement rather than cooperating.

## 4. Clarity

*Deficit definition:* The speaker's turn exhibits a mismatch between their expressed epistemic confidence and the epistemic state the interaction has actually established — the speaker proceeds as though something is shared, settled, or understood when it is not, or fails to articulate what is needed for shared understanding to advance. The label attaches to the speaker whose turn displays the miscalibration, not to the other party or to the interaction abstractly. Clarity is restricted to the content dimension: procedural clarity — unclear roles, next steps, participation expectations — is Agency (§2), not Clarity.

### 4.1 Deficit signals

Properties of the speaker's own utterance. The unifying feature of Clarity signals is **epistemic miscalibration**: the speaker's turn exhibits a level of confidence, specificity, or commitment that does not match what the observable interaction supports. Miscalibration runs in two directions — the speaker may treat unsettled matters as resolved (overcertainty), or fail to provide the specificity needed for shared understanding to advance (undercertainty). Both directions produce the same downstream failure: the parties' working understanding of the substantive issues diverges without either party registering the divergence.

The signals below are organized into two clusters: **commitment without warrant** (the speaker treats open or contested matters as settled) and **insufficient specification** (the speaker's contribution is too vague, inconsistent, or incongruent to support shared understanding).

#### Commitment without warrant

**Unverified intent attribution.** The speaker's turn references the other party's motive, meaning, or intention as though established, when the other party has not confirmed that reading. Observable as: the speaker states or implies what the other party was "trying to do," "really meant," or "wanted," and proceeds on the basis of that attribution without seeking confirmation. The attribution is treated as a premise for the speaker's subsequent reasoning, not as a hypothesis to be checked.

This is a high-precision Clarity signal (cf. FPO's "silent intent fixing" concept, translated here to observable turn-level features). The speaker forecloses alternative interpretations of an ambiguous situation — the interpretive question is treated as closed when it is open. **Discriminator from Reciprocity:** when intent-fixing serves a positional argument (the speaker locks in an interpretation that advantages their position, co-occurring with §3.1 positional or attack signals), assess for Reciprocity. When intent-fixing reflects epistemic foreclosure (the speaker appears unaware that alternative readings exist, or proceeds as though the reading is obvious), it is Clarity. Backward-looking test: does the speaker's prior window show positional holding and competitive orientation (Reciprocity) or effortful but unchecked inference (Clarity)? See §4.5 for the full boundary case.

**Overcertainty on underspecified matters.** The speaker's turn expresses confidence or commitment on a point the interaction has not yet established. Observable as: the speaker treats an ambiguous reference as resolved, asserts a factual claim the dialogue has not verified, or proceeds with a specific interpretation of a contested point as though the other party shares it. The marker is not the speaker's subjective certainty but the gap between the speaker's expressed confidence and what the prior exchange supports.

This is the complement of "vague and general language" below — both reflect the same underlying miscalibration, in opposite directions.

**Inference overshoot.** The speaker's turn exhibits a conclusion that has advanced beyond what the observable interaction has established. Across the window, the speaker's turns show a trajectory from observation-proximate claims toward interpretive conclusions, without intervening verification or checking (cf. the Ladder of Inference concept, Senge/Argyris). Observable as: the current turn contains an interpretive or evaluative conclusion whose intermediate inferential steps — from observed behavior to added meaning to generalized judgment — were never tested against the other party's understanding. The speaker's conclusion sits at the top of an inference chain whose links were not verified.

Window-level signal: the speaker's earlier turns may contain more data-proximate observations; the current turn has jumped to a conclusion without the intermediate steps having been checked. The annotator assesses whether the current turn's conclusion is warranted by what the speaker (and the other party) have actually established in the prior exchange.

#### Insufficient specification

When the speaker openly names their inability to articulate rather than papering over the gap, this is not a deficit — see §4.1 Exclusions below.

**Vague and general language where specificity is needed.** The speaker's turn uses imprecise, exaggerated, or generalized language where the interaction requires concrete specifics. Observable as: "you aren't doing your share" (vague) where the speaker could have said "you didn't clean the kitchen last night" (specific); "you never get your work done on time" (exaggerated) where the speaker could have said "this is the third paper you've handed in late" (accurate). The speaker's contribution is too imprecise to allow the other party to engage with the specific content at issue.

This is a lower-precision signal than the commitment-without-warrant signals because vague language may reflect stylistic habits, emotional flooding, or early-stage problem articulation rather than an epistemic deficit. It becomes diagnostic when it persists across the window despite the other party's attempts to engage with specifics, or when it co-occurs with other §4.1 signals.

**Self-contradiction across the window.** The speaker asserts a position in the current turn that is inconsistent with a position the same speaker asserted in a prior turn, without acknowledging the shift. Observable as: the speaker's current claim contradicts or is logically incompatible with their own earlier claim, and the speaker does not flag the change, explain it, or reconcile the two positions. A single instance may reflect genuine revision or imprecise expression; a pattern of unacknowledged self-contradictions across the window is a stronger signal.

**Discriminator for cross-party contradiction:** When the speaker's current turn contradicts the *other party's* prior stated position without engaging it, the primary assessment is Reciprocity (non-responsive counter-attack, §3.1) or Acknowledgement (failing to register what was said, §1.1), not Clarity. The discriminator is whose prior commitment is being contradicted: self-contradiction → Clarity; contradicting the other's position without engaging it → assess Reciprocity/Acknowledgement first. See §4.5 for the boundary case.

**Incongruence within the speaker's turn.** The speaker's stated content is inconsistent with their own non-verbal or paraverbal signals within the same turn. Observable as: the speaker says they are comfortable while exhibiting distress cues, says they are not angry in an angry register, or asserts satisfaction while physically withdrawing. The contradiction is between the speaker's own channels, not between the speaker and the other party.

Distinct from Acknowledgement invalidating non-verbals (§3.1, other-directed invalidating non-verbal pattern): in incongruence, the *speaker's own* signals contradict each other. In invalidating non-verbals, the speaker's cues are directed at withdrawing recognition from the *other party's* contribution.

When the speaker names the mismatch within the same turn, this is not a deficit — see §4.4 (self-flagged incongruence).

#### Exclusions

**Accurate epistemic signaling — not a deficit.** When a speaker cannot articulate something and openly names this inability, the turn does not exhibit a Clarity deficit. The distinguishing feature is whether the speaker registers the gap or proceeds as though it does not exist. "I don't know what that is" or "I don't have a next part" — stated without false resolution — is accurate calibration of expressed confidence to actual epistemic state, which is the opposite of the miscalibration that defines Clarity. The deficit attaches when the speaker cannot articulate X *and does not acknowledge this*, or when the speaker papers over the gap with false specificity, premature closure, or surface agreement. A speaker who flags their own uncertainty in real time is performing epistemic maintenance, not exhibiting a deficit.

This exclusion is especially relevant when the inability to articulate co-occurs with Acknowledgement signals. A speaker who cannot find words for an emotionally live concern may appear to exhibit insufficient specification, but if the articulation failure is downstream of an unmet recognition need — the speaker suppresses or truncates because they expect non-registration, not because they lack the conceptual resources — the primary deficit is Acknowledgement (§1.1), and the apparent Clarity gap is a symptom rather than an independent deficit. Apply the §4.5 (Clarity vs. Acknowledgement) discriminator: does the speaker's lead-up show escalating bids for recognition (Acknowledgement-primary) or effortful attempts to convey substantive content (Clarity-primary)?

### 4.2 Diagnostic lead-up across the window

The accumulated pattern across the speaker's prior turns that grounds the judgment for the latest turn.

**Advancing inference without verification across the speaker's turns.** The speaker's turns show a trajectory from observation-proximate statements toward interpretive conclusions, with no intervening turns in which the speaker checks, asks, or tests their developing interpretation against the other party's understanding. The current turn's conclusion is the culmination of an inference chain built across multiple turns without verification at any step.

This pattern grounds both inference-overshoot and unverified-intent-attribution signals in §4.1: the window reveals *how* the speaker arrived at the current turn's unwarranted conclusion.

**Absence of summarizing or checking in the speaker's turns.** Across the window, the speaker has not attempted to recap, paraphrase, or verify their understanding of the other party's position. No turns in the speaker's history contain "let me make sure I understand," reformulations of the other's position, or explicit checking questions. This is an absence-based signal — the non-occurrence of a behavior rather than its presence — and is **necessary but not sufficient**: absence of checking alone does not diagnose Clarity. It becomes diagnostic when combined with §4.1 signals (particularly inference overshoot or unverified intent attribution) in the current turn.

**Persistent miscalibration despite the other party's corrective signals.** The other party's prior turns contain corrections, clarifications, or challenges to the speaker's framing, and the speaker's subsequent turns do not adjust. The speaker continues with the same unchecked interpretation, vague language, or contradictory framing despite available corrective information in the lead-up. This pattern distinguishes persistent Clarity deficit from momentary imprecision.

### 4.3 Turn 0 handling

**Default: N.** The opening turn epistemic constraint is most acute for Clarity. A Clarity deficit requires evidence of a gap *between the parties' understandings* — and at turn 0, there is no interactional basis for the annotator to assess whether such a gap exists. Vague language may reflect the speaker's opening framing; overcertainty may reflect legitimate prior knowledge the annotator cannot see; intent attribution may reference a shared history the annotator has no access to. In all cases, the annotator cannot distinguish a Clarity deficit from a gap between the parties and the annotator.

**Essentially no exceptions.** Unlike the other three components, Clarity has no reliable surface-unambiguous signal at turn 0. The signals that are most diagnostic — inference overshoot, unverified intent attribution, persistent vagueness, self-contradiction — all require a window of prior interaction to establish either the miscalibration or the pattern. Even explicit self-contradiction requires at least two assertions to be visible, which means at minimum two turns from the same speaker.

Intra-turn incongruence (verbal/non-verbal mismatch within the opening turn) is the closest candidate for a turn-0 exception, but in isolation it is ambiguous: it could indicate a Clarity deficit, emotional flooding, nervousness, or a speaker who has not yet settled into the interaction. Label N and note the incongruence for reassessment once subsequent turns provide context.

### 4.4 Resolution indicators

Four cases, organized by whose prior turn is being repaired and what the current turn exhibits. All indicators are intra-turn or backward-looking.

**Post-repair confirmation (the speaker's prior Clarity deficit was addressed by the other party; the current turn shows evidence of corrected understanding).**
- The speaker explicitly corrects or revises a prior unverified attribution: "Oh — you weren't trying to cut me off, you just needed to close that account for another reason." The speaker names the prior misunderstanding and replaces it with a checked interpretation.
- The speaker shifts from vague or exaggerated language to descriptive specificity: moves from "you never" to concrete instances, from interpretive claims to observation-proximate language.
- The speaker acknowledges a self-contradiction and reconciles it: "I said X earlier, but what I actually mean is Y."
- The speaker agrees on the factual content at issue, even if they continue to disagree about its implications (which would shift the relevant deficit to another component — most often Reciprocity if the disagreement is about positions, or Acknowledgement if the disagreement is about the significance of the speaker's experience).
- **Labeling:** The current turn is labeled N for Clarity.

**Speaker-as-repairer (the current speaker is performing repair on the other party's prior Clarity deficit).** The speaker's turn exhibits clarification-seeking, verification-seeking, or explicit ambiguity-naming directed at the other party's prior unchecked claims. Observable as (cf. FPO's information-gain concept, translated to turn-level features):
- The speaker asks a question that targets an identified ambiguity or underspecification in the other party's prior turn: "When you say 'not doing my share,' do you mean around the house or at work?"
- The speaker requests verification of a specific claim or attribution the other party has made: "You said I was trying to cut you off — is that what you think I was doing, or is something else going on?"
- The speaker explicitly names an ambiguity or contradiction in the other party's prior turns: "Earlier you said X, and now you're saying Y — I want to make sure I understand which one you mean."
- The speaker summarizes or paraphrases the other party's position and asks for confirmation: "So what I'm hearing is that your main concern is Z — is that right?"
- The speaker uses descriptive rather than evaluative language to restate the other party's claim, converting a vague or judgmental frame into concrete terms.
- **Labeling:** This turn is labeled N for Clarity regardless of whether the repair lands in subsequent turns (scope rule 2 + 3). The label is based on the intra-turn repair-action signals, not on the other party's future response.

**Self-flagged incongruence (the speaker catches and names their own signal mismatch within the same turn).** When the speaker identifies and names the contradiction between their own signals — e.g., offering reassurance and then acknowledging that their internal state contradicts it — the incongruence is being surfaced rather than concealed. This is intra-turn self-repair: the speaker has caught the gap without requiring the other party's intervention. A speaker who says "I want to say I can hold this, but I'm also relieved you stopped, and I don't know what to do with that" is performing real-time self-correction.

The exclusion applies only when the speaker names the mismatch within the same turn. If the incongruence is visible to the annotator but not flagged by the speaker — reassurance delivered in a contradictory register without any acknowledgment of the tension — the §4.1 deficit signal stands. The test is simple: does the speaker show awareness that their signals are in tension? If yes, not a deficit. If no, assess for Clarity.

- **Labeling:** Label N for Clarity.

**Accommodation and withdrawal — not repair.** When Clarity deficit signals cease *without* post-repair confirmation signals, the cessation is not repair. Intra-turn markers:

- The speaker has stopped exhibiting inference overshoot, unverified attribution, or vague language, but has not replaced these with checked interpretations, descriptive specificity, or explicit reconciliation of contradictions.
- The speaker produces surface agreement or procedural compliance without substantive engagement with the content at issue: agrees without specifying what they are agreeing to, moves on without resolving the discrepancy.
- No explicit correction, revision, or reconciliation of prior miscalibrated claims.

*Discriminator from genuine post-repair confirmation.* In genuine confirmation, the speaker's turn exhibits at least one positive resolution signal (explicit correction, shift to descriptive language, reconciliation of contradiction, checked interpretation). In accommodation, the miscalibrated signals have ceased but the underlying epistemic gap has not been addressed — the speaker has stopped *expressing* the miscalibration without having *corrected* it.

*Annotation handling.* Label N for Clarity — the speaker is no longer exhibiting §4.1 Clarity signals — and flag for assessment of an emerging different deficit. The most common trajectory: the speaker may be transitioning into Agency (if the accommodation reflects giving up on understanding rather than achieving it — check for §2.1 signals), or into Reciprocity (if the speaker moves from epistemic confusion to positional assertion without having resolved the underlying content dispute — check for §3.1 signals).

### 4.5 Boundary cases

**Clarity vs. Reciprocity (epistemic foreclosure vs. strategic foreclosure).**

Both components can produce a speaker who treats open questions as settled. The distinction is whether the speaker's foreclosure is epistemic (the speaker does not realize or acknowledge the ambiguity — Clarity) or strategic (the speaker is advancing a fixed position — Reciprocity). This boundary is also documented from the Reciprocity side in §3.5 (Reciprocity vs. Clarity, withholding vs. confusion); the discriminators are consistent across both directions.

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Clarity-primary:** The speaker's turn exhibits unverified intent attribution, overcertainty, or inference overshoot *without* co-occurring positional or attack signals (§3.1). The speaker's prior turns show effortful but unchecked inference — the speaker is trying to understand but has settled on an interpretation without verification. The speaker appears unaware that alternative readings exist.
- **Reciprocity-primary:** The speaker's turn exhibits intent-fixing or overcertainty *with* co-occurring positional language, other-directed attack signals, or a pattern of positional escalation in the lead-up (§3.1). The speaker locks in an interpretation that advantages their position. The speaker's prior turns show competitive orientation rather than epistemic effort.
- **The withholding/confusion sub-case.** Information asymmetry may reflect Reciprocity withholding (the speaker possesses relevant information and chooses not to surface it) or Clarity confusion (the speaker is genuinely unable to articulate or surface the information). Backward-looking test (same as §3.5): has the information been requested and deflected (Reciprocity) or simply not surfaced by either party (possibly Clarity)?
- **Co-occurrence.** When both patterns are present — unchecked inference *and* positional holding — flag both. Priority makes Reciprocity primary and Clarity secondary.

**Clarity vs. Acknowledgement (the content trap).**

A content dispute may actually be driven by unmet recognition needs. Repetition can be driven by an information gap (Clarity) or by an unmet need to be heard (Acknowledgement). This boundary is also documented from the Acknowledgement side in §1.5 (Acknowledgement vs. Clarity); the discriminators are the same from both directions.

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Acknowledgement-primary:** The speaker's repetition co-occurs with §1.1 signals (self-minimizing, universalizing appeals defending the self, grievance cataloguing with self-as-wronged framing). The speaker's prior turns show escalating bids for recognition. The other party has already attempted clarification in the prior window and the speaker's repetition continued unchanged — the information was addressed but the emotional need was not.
- **Clarity-primary:** The speaker's repetition co-occurs with §4.1 signals (vague language, inference overshoot, overcertainty on underspecified matters). The speaker's prior turns show effortful attempts to articulate a substantive point that has not landed. No clarification has been attempted by either party, or the speaker's repetition shows adjustment in response to the other's engagement (restating with different emphasis, adding specifics) rather than unchanged recycling.
- **Signal-profile test (backward-looking):** Has the other party already attempted clarification or interest-summary in the prior window? If clarification was attempted and the speaker's repetition continued unchanged, the deficit is Acknowledgement. If interest-summary was attempted and the speaker's tension de-escalated, the prior deficit was Acknowledgement (now resolved — post-repair confirmation per §1.4). If neither has been attempted, fall back to the co-occurring signal profile.
- **Co-occurrence.** When both patterns persist — the speaker shows both recognition-seeking and epistemic confusion — flag both. Priority makes Acknowledgement primary and Clarity secondary. The diagnostic priority means Clarity is assessed only after Acknowledgement has been considered. Resolving the Acknowledgement deficit often dissolves the apparent Clarity gap.

**Clarity vs. Agency (epistemic confusion vs. exclusion-driven information gap).**

Both components can produce a speaker who lacks information needed for substantive participation. The distinction is whether the information gap is a standalone epistemic failure (Clarity) or a symptom of the speaker's exclusion from a process where that information would have been shared (Agency). This boundary is also documented from the Agency side in §2.5 (Agency vs. Clarity); the discriminators are consistent across both directions.

*Intra-turn and backward-looking discriminators:*
- **Agency-primary:** The speaker's lead-up shows markers of exclusion — references to not being included, descriptions of a process they were outside of, progressive deferral or constraint narratives co-occurring with the information gap. The information gap is embedded in a broader pattern of foreclosed participation. The speaker's uncertainty presents not as effortful confusion but as foreclosure: "How would I know? Nobody told me anything."
- **Clarity-primary:** The speaker's lead-up shows effortful attempts to understand — asking questions, attempting to articulate, working through confusion. No markers of exclusion or foreclosed participation. The speaker appears willing and able to engage but unable to resolve the substantive confusion. The information is genuinely unknown or unclear to both parties and needs to be established jointly.
- **The "I don't know what I want" sub-case (same as §2.5).** Genuine not-knowing without powerless speech or constraint language → Clarity. Not-knowing accompanied by deferral, constraint narratives, or "what's the point" framing → Agency. When both patterns are present, apply both labels; Agency is primary per diagnostic priority.
- **Procedural intent-fixing sub-case.** When the speaker treats an open procedural question as settled (assumes the other agreed to a process they didn't agree to), assess for Agency if the speaker is foreclosing the other's process participation, and for Clarity if the speaker is genuinely confused about what was agreed. The discriminator is the speaker's orientation: is the speaker advancing a procedural fait accompli (Agency) or operating on an unchecked assumption about shared procedural understanding (Clarity)?

### 4.6 Gold standard candidates

**Candidate 1: Unverified intent attribution with repair (two-party exchange).**
Turn A (Speaker 1): "You closed the account so I couldn't buy food — you were cutting me off." The speaker attributes motive to the other party (intent to cut off) based on the effect of their action (couldn't buy food), without seeking confirmation. Clarity deficit: unverified intent attribution (§4.1). Turn B (Speaker 2): "Wait — why do you think I was cutting you off? I closed it because the bank required a new joint account form." The speaker asks about the attribution and offers an alternative explanation — speaker-as-repairer (§4.4), labeled N. Turn C (Speaker 1): "Oh — you weren't trying to cut me off, you just needed to close that account for another reason." Post-repair confirmation (§4.4), labeled N. A complete deficit-to-resolution trajectory across three turns.

**Candidate 2: Intra-turn incongruence (single turn).**
The speaker says "I'm not angry about it at all" while exhibiting an angry vocal register (raised volume, clipped speech, tense posture). The speaker's verbal content contradicts their own paraverbal signals within the same turn. Clarity deficit: incongruence (§4.1). A short, high-precision example suitable for a single-turn label.

**Candidate 3: Inference overshoot across the window.**
Turn A: "She didn't reply to my email for two days." (Observation-proximate.) Turn B: "She's obviously avoiding me." (Added meaning — no checking.) Turn C: "She doesn't respect me enough to even respond." (Evaluative conclusion — inference chain completed without verification.) The speaker's conclusion in Turn C has advanced well beyond what the interaction has established. Clarity deficit on Turn C: inference overshoot (§4.1), grounded by the advancing-inference-without-verification pattern (§4.2).

**Candidate 4: Overcertainty on underspecified matter.**
The speaker says "We both know this arrangement isn't working" when the other party has not indicated dissatisfaction with the arrangement. The speaker treats a contested point as shared knowledge. Clarity deficit: overcertainty on an underspecified matter (§4.1). The signal is the gap between the speaker's "we both know" framing and the absence of any prior confirmation from the other party.

**Candidate 5: The content trap — Acknowledgement vs. Clarity.**
Turn A: Speaker states a concern. Turn B: Other party explains the factual basis. Turn C: Speaker restates the same concern with escalating affect, unchanged by the explanation. The explanation addressed the content; the repetition signals unmet recognition. If the speaker's Turn C co-occurs with §1.1 signals (self-as-wronged framing, universalizing appeals) and the repetition is unchanged by the clarification, the deficit is Acknowledgement, not Clarity. This exemplifies the §4.5 boundary discriminator.
