# Core, M. G., & Allen, J. F. (1997). Coding dialogs with the DAMSL annotation scheme.

## Citation

Core, M. G., & Allen, J. F. (1997). Coding dialogs with the DAMSL annotation scheme. In *Proceedings of the AAAI Fall Symposium on Communicative Action in Humans and Machines* (pp. 28–35). AAAI Press.

## Abstract

This paper describes the DAMSL annotation scheme for communicative acts in dialog. The scheme has three layers: Forward Communicative Functions, Backward Communicative Functions, and Utterance Features. Each layer allows multiple communicative functions of an utterance to be labeled. The Forward Communicative Functions consist of a taxonomy in a similar style as the actions of traditional speech act theory. The Backward Communicative Functions indicate how the current utterance relates to the previous dialog, such as accepting a proposal, confirming understanding, or answering a question. The Utterance Fea- tures include information about an utterance's form and content, such as whether an utterance concerns the communication process itself or deals with the subject at hand. The kappa inter-annotator reliability scores for the firrst test of DAMSL with human annotators show promise, but are on average 0.15 lower than the accepted kappa scores for such annotations. How- ever, the slight revisions to DAMSL discussed here should increase accuracy on the next set of tests and produce a reliable, exible, and comprehensive utterance annotation scheme.

## Project Relevance

Core and Allen introduce DAMSL (Dialogue Act Markup in Several Layers), which established the precedent of applying multiple, simultaneously applicable functional labels to a single dialogue turn. DAMSL's forward-looking and backward-looking dimension structure — labeling what a turn does to the dialogue state versus how it responds to prior turns — directly informs the structural logic of schemas that must assign multiple co-occurring labels at the turn level.
