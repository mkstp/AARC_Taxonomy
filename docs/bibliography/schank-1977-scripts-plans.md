# Schank, R. C., & Abelson, R. P. (1977). Scripts, plans, goals, and understanding.

## Citation

Schank, R. C., & Abelson, R. P. (1977). *Scripts, plans, goals, and understanding: An inquiry into human knowledge structures.* Lawrence Erlbaum Associates.

## Abstract

[PENDING — book; no publisher abstract. Verifiable summary: Schank and Abelson propose that human understanding of routine social situations is organised around "scripts" — stereotyped sequences of actions that specify roles, props, scenes, and expected orderings. Scripts are activated by contextual cues and allow comprehenders to infer unstated actions and fill narrative gaps without explicit reasoning. The central claim is that structured knowledge dominates understanding: comprehension of social action is primarily a matter of retrieving and applying pre-existing representational structures. Beyond scripts, the book develops a hierarchy of knowledge structures — plans and goals — for reasoning about novel situations where no stereotyped script applies. Source: verified via Taylor & Francis/Routledge catalogue and Internet Archive; digital copy available.]

## Project Relevance

Grounds the decision to treat each persona as a script-following agent with stable, structured behavior rather than a free-form generator. The persona schema — primary TKI mode, beliefs about self and others, activation profile, storm shift — constitutes a character script in Schank and Abelson's sense: a structured set of role behaviors, interpretive defaults, and expected transitions that specify how the persona will behave across the range of situational triggers the scenario produces. This framing is cognitively realistic: people themselves operate largely from scripts in recurrent social situations, including conflict. It is also computationally productive: treating the persona as a script converts the director's authenticity-checking task from open-ended evaluation (is this turn plausible?) to schema-constrained verification (is this turn consistent with this character's declared working models, TKI mode, and escalation trajectory?). For the computational precedent for a two-level architecture that explicitly balances character-script behavior against author-level coherence, see `riedl-2010-narrative-planning.md`. *Verify: library copy or Internet Archive copy of Schank & Abelson (1977), Lawrence Erlbaum Associates.*
