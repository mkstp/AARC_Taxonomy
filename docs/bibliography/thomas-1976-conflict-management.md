# Thomas, K. W. (1976). Conflict and conflict management.

## Citation

Thomas, K. W. (1976). Conflict and conflict management. In M. D. Dunnette (Ed.), *Handbook of industrial and organizational psychology* (pp. 889–935). Rand McNally.

Verifiable summary: The chapter reviews conflict theory across organizational, social-psychological, and negotiation traditions and proposes a two-dimensional model of conflict-handling behavior based on assertiveness (concern for one's own outcomes) and cooperativeness (concern for the other party's outcomes). The intersection of the two dimensions yields five modes — competing, collaborating, compromising, avoiding, and accommodating — each representing a distinct behavioral orientation. The chapter adapts Blake and Mouton's managerial grid, reframing its axes in behavioral rather than attitudinal terms, and provides the theoretical foundation for the Thomas–Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument (Thomas & Kilmann, 1974). ## Project Relevance

Grounds the persona design decision to assign each character a primary TKI mode. The five modes are a closed set of behavioral dispositions derived from the crossing of two orthogonal axes; they are observable in dialogue rather than merely reported on a questionnaire. For the synthetic corpus, this allows a persona's conflict-handling mode to be expressed as a consistent interactional style — a competing persona asserts claims and resists concession; an avoiding persona deflects or withdraws — that the director-LLM can check against for behavioral fidelity.
