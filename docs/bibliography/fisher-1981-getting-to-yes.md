## Project Relevance

Fisher and Ury establish the foundational distinction between positions — the stated demands parties advance in negotiation — and interests — the underlying needs, concerns, and values those demands represent. Principled negotiation, their central contribution, reorients dispute resolution away from positional bargaining toward collaborative identification of mutually satisfying outcomes. This framework is the intellectual ancestor of the mutual gains approach that Susskind and Field develop, and can be interpreted as conceptual grounding for the AARC model's process and relationship components: procedural fairness and reciprocal engagement are conditions under which parties may move from positional entrenchment to interest-level dialogue. Including this source completes the dispute resolution lineage the bibliography establishes. *Note: annotation is intentionally narrow — paper cites Fisher and Ury for the positional/interest distinction only, not for the full principled negotiation framework.*
