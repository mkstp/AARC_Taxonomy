# Pruitt, D. G. (2015). The evolution of readiness theory.

## Citation

Pruitt, D. G. (2015). The evolution of readiness theory. In M. Galluccio (Ed.), *Handbook of international negotiation: Interpersonal, intercultural, and diplomatic perspectives* (pp. 123–138). Springer.

## Abstract

This is a step-by-step account of the development of readiness theory, which concerns how a disputant decides to enter negotiation aimed at settling an intractable conflict. A cumulative case study method was used, involving sequential examination of the peace processes that settled three conflicts between dominant and subordinate ethnic groups. The theory that emerged from these case studies embraces the following topics among others: motivation to end the conflict, perceived urgency of escaping the conflict, optimism about the outcome of negotiation, secret prenegotiation communication between the disputants, mutually acceptable substantive frameworks, and positive vs. negative interdependence.

## Project Relevance

Pruitt's readiness theory establishes that productive negotiation requires each party to hold two concurrent perceptions: motivation to pursue a negotiated settlement, and optimism that the counterpart will reciprocate conciliatory moves. The second condition — what Pruitt terms optimism about the other side — corresponds directly to AARC's Reciprocity component understood as a precondition for engagement rather than merely a behavioral property of turns. In the position paper, readiness theory is cited as independent scholarly convergence on the AARC diagnostic hierarchy: a Reciprocity deficit, in readiness theory terms, is the belief that conciliation will not be met in kind — a structural condition that warrants attention before content-level correction is likely to be effective. *Verify: page range (pp. 123–138) in Galluccio (Ed.), Springer, 2015 before submission.*
