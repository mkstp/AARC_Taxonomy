## Abstract

[PENDING — book; no publisher abstract. Verifiable summary: Pruitt and Rubin present an integrative theory of social conflict organized around three phases — escalation, stalemate, and settlement — and argue that escalation is driven by identifiable psychological and structural mechanisms that can be characterized at both individual and dyadic levels. They identify five transformations that conflicts undergo as they escalate: light tactics give way to heavy tactics; specific issues proliferate; concerns shift from particular to general; motivations move from doing well, to winning, to hurting the other; and the number of involved parties expands. At the individual level, they identify selective perception, self-fulfilling prophecies, autistic hostility, and overcommitment as mechanisms by which parties become progressively less responsive to disconfirming information. ## Project Relevance

Grounds the `activation_profile` and `storm_shift` fields in the persona schema. Pruitt and Rubin provide the conceptual vocabulary for describing a persona's characteristic escalation trajectory: what triggers movement from light to heavy tactics, whether issue proliferation is likely, whether the persona tends toward doing-well, winning, or hurting, and which individual-level mechanism dominates their distortion pattern under sustained pressure. This allows individual differences in escalation to be specified as a position on well-defined theoretical dimensions rather than as free-form prose, making the director's behavioral fidelity check tractable.
