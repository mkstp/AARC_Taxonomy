# Hoben, M. (2020). Cultivating dignity in a year of rupture.

## Citation

Hoben, M. (2020, October 5). Cultivating dignity in a year of rupture. *Consensus Building Institute.*

## Abstract

Over the last several years, with the help of my colleagues1, I have refined a frame of reference (or lens) that has helped me design multi-party processes centered on dignity more effectively. I call this the AARC of dignified dialogue – comprised of Acknowledgement, Agency, Reciprocity, and Clarity. This framework evolved in my practice as I encountered more and more conflicts that were being fueled by deep differences in identity and lived experience, as well as histories of power imbalances and discrimination. The following four litmus questions help me enhance design and gauge potential for “dignity in dialogue.” They don’t solve all the structural imbalances and challenges that might be present2, yet – when given sufficient attention – they open up the possibility of more transformative, problem-solving conversations.

“Are the people affected feeling that their concerns and aspirations have been deeply heard and acknowledged?”
“Do they have a sense of agency in terms of how they will solve their problems, and on what terms?”
“Are they experiencing and contributing to reciprocal engagement as a constructive exchange – both in words and action?”
“Do they feel a sense of clarity about the process itself – that is, do they understand what will come next, can they access that engagement fairly, and how will those steps address their deepest concerns?”

## Project Relevance

This practitioner piece by the author of the AARC model connects dignity theory to CBI's applied conflict resolution methodology. It serves as the primary bridge between Hicks's theoretical work and the operationalized AARC framework used as the top-down annotation layer in the synthetic data pipeline. The source is a practitioner blog post rather than peer-reviewed literature; the AARC model has not been formally validated in prior academic work. Part of this project's contribution is to operationalize and empirically test what is currently a practitioner heuristic, and this provenance should be acknowledged in the writeup.
