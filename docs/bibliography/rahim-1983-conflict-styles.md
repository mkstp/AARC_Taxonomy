# Rahim, M. A. (1983). A measure of styles of handling interpersonal conflict.

## Citation

Rahim, M. A. (1983). A measure of styles of handling interpersonal conflict. *Academy of Management Journal*, *26*(2), 368–376.

## Abstract

The Rahim Organization Conflict Inventory-II was developed to measure 5 styles of handling interpersonal conflict: avoiding, obliging, dominating, integrating, and compromising. Factor analysis of data from 1,219 executives clearly reflected the 5 a priori dimensions of conflict styles. The test–retest and internal consistency reliablility coefficients for the 5 scales were satisfactory and compared favorably with other existing instruments. Four of the scales were free from social desirability or response distortion bias. (21 ref) (PsycINFO Database Record (c) 2016 APA, all rights reserved)

## Project Relevance

Introduces the dual-concern model of conflict styles, organized along two axes: concern for self (assertiveness) and concern for others (cooperativeness). The dominating/competing style maps most directly onto assertive, directed intervention — the party or intervener prioritizes its own epistemic or normative position and resists the counterpart's framing. This is relevant because FPO's challenge and refuse actions both express high concern-for-self in Rahim's terms, providing a theoretical rationale for why these actions belong to a distinct strategic register from integrative or clarifying moves. *Note: Rahim's model was developed for organizational behavior between humans; its transferability to AI-mediated interaction should not be assumed without qualification.*
