# Hicks, D. (2011). Dignity: Its essential role in resolving conflict.

## Citation

Hicks, D. (2011). *Dignity: Its essential role in resolving conflict.* Yale University Press.

## Abstract

Drawing on her extensive experience in international conflict resolution and on insights from evolutionary biology, psychology, and neuroscience, Donna Hicks explains what the elements of dignity are, how to recognize dignity violations, how to respond when we are not treated with dignity, how dignity can restore a broken relationship, why leaders must understand the concept of dignity, and more. By choosing dignity as a way of life, Hicks shows, we open the way to greater peace within ourselves and to a safer and more humane world for all.

## Project Relevance

Hicks provides the theoretical foundation for treating dignity as a primary driver of conflict escalation and impasse. This work grounds the AARC model's emphasis on acknowledgment and agency as intervention targets — both of which can be understood as operationalizations of dignity preservation at the turn level. In the intervention taxonomy, Hicks informs how identity-based deficits are categorized and why certain interventions (those that restore or affirm a party's sense of worth) are prioritized over purely epistemic corrections. *Note: annotation is diagnostic in framing; paper uses Hicks specifically for the precondition claim — that dignity-based failures block productive engagement — rather than for Hicks's broader framework.*
