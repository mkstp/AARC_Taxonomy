## Project Relevance

This practitioner training manual is the source for the CPRI framework (Content, Process, Relationship, Identity) as extended from Wilmot and Hocker's three-component model. It is cited specifically for the pedagogical concept of the "content trap" — the tendency for practitioners to over-index on substantive dispute content while relational, procedural, and identity-based tensions remain unresolved. This directly motivates the argument that an exclusively epistemic approach to friction detection replicates the content trap at the computational level. As a training manual rather than peer-reviewed literature, it carries practitioner authority rather than scholarly authority; this should be reflected in how the source is characterized in the writeup. *Note: Wilmot & Hocker (1997) cover Content, Process, and Relationship; Identity was added by the Justice Institute. The attribution distinction is visible to a careful reader and should be explicit in the paper.*
