## Project Relevance

Wilmot and Hocker establish the core theoretical premise that conflict operates simultaneously across multiple dimensions — Content, Process, and Relationship — and that privileging any single dimension risks misdiagnosis. This text is a standard reference in the conflict resolution field and lends disciplinary authority to the multidimensional framing the writeup applies to FPO's scope limitations. The four-dimensional extension adding Identity, and the associated pedagogical concept of the "content trap," are attributed to the Justice Institute of British Columbia (2021), which builds on this framework in a practitioner training context. *Note: the paper cites Wilmot and Hocker for 4 CPRI goals in Para 13, but the source only covers 3 (Content, Process, Relationship); Identity was added by the Justice Institute. This attribution inconsistency is visible to a careful reader and must be corrected in the writeup.*
