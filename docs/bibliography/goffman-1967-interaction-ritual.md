## Project Relevance

Goffman establishes face — the positive social value a person claims for themselves in interaction — and face-work — the communicative actions taken to maintain one's own and others' face — as fundamental organizing principles of social interaction. This is the foundational text on which Ting-Toomey's face-negotiation theory (1988) directly builds; citing the intercultural extension without the original weakens the theoretical chain. The concept of face-threat is directly relevant to the Identity component of AARC and to why identity-layer failures require specialized intervention: epistemic correction of a party's stated position can simultaneously constitute a face-threat, triggering defensive escalation that the correction itself does not resolve.
